After *Die Another Day* drowned the series in invisible cars and ice palaces, the producers did the only honourable thing: burned it down and started over. *Casino Royale* is Bond with the polish sanded off and the nerve endings left exposed.

## The plot

Freshly promoted to double-0 status, Bond tracks a bomb-maker through Madagascar, then settles in for the main event: a high-stakes poker game at the Casino Royale in Montenegro, where banker-to-terrorists Le Chiffre is trying to win back money that belongs to some very unsympathetic clients. The film devotes genuine tension to a card game. A *card game*. That takes confidence.

## The villain

Le Chiffre, played by Mads Mikkelsen with a weeping-bleeding eye and an inhaler, is terrifying precisely because he's *afraid*. He's not a megalomaniac with a volcano base — he's an accountant for very bad people, in debt over his head, desperate and dripping. The chair scene is one of the most uncomfortable stretches in the entire series.

## The girl

The Bond girl of the night is **Vesper Lynd** — HM Treasury analyst, reluctant companion, and the woman this whole era is secretly about. Eva Green rewires the entire formula: she reads Bond over dinner, trades barbs as an equal, and breaks his heart on a Venetian staircase. Craig's Bond spends the rest of the franchise carrying what she did to him, and the films are better for it. No Bond girl before or since lands like Vesper.

## The verdict

That first free-running chase through the construction site announced a new Bond: one who bleeds, grunts, and gets it wrong before he gets it right. The tuxedo doesn't arrive until the final act — "Bond. James Bond." — and by then it's an earned title, not a costume.

**Filed:** 5/5 — the correct reboot, delivered four decades in.
