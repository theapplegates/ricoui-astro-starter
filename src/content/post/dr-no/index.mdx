Go back to 1962 and watch the blueprint get drawn with a straight razor. *Dr. No* is smaller, slower, and stranger than what followed — a detective story that happens to end with a nuclear reactor. And yet every Bond since is still quoting it.

## The plot

A British station chief vanishes in Kingston, and the new man from London — one James Bond, gambling at a chemin-de-fer table with a cigarette and a legendary introduction — is sent to investigate. The trail leads to Crab Key, a private island with a polite host, a dragon, and a plan to disrupt American rocket launches from Cape Canaveral.

## The villain

Dr. Julius No barely appears until the third act, but the dinner scene with Joseph Wiseman is immaculate: cultured menace, black tunic, hands of polished steel hidden in gloves. "The successful criminal brain is always superior. It has to be." He believed it. The series built six decades of villains on this one dinner.

## The girl

The original Bond girl of the night: **Honey Ryder**, played by Ursula Andress, emerging from the surf with a knife on her belt and shells to sell. It is arguably the most famous entrance in cinema history — the shot everything else in the series is secretly in conversation with. Honey is a survivor, her own woman long before Bond arrives, and Andress plays her with an easy competence the role didn't strictly require and absolutely benefited from.

## The verdict

Connery's Bond here is still wet clay — harder, hungrier, less amused by himself than he'd become. The gun barrel opens, the theme plays for the first time, and sixty-four years later we're still in the theatre.

**Filed:** 4/5 — respect the origin story.
