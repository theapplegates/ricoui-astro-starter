Two years after *Star Wars*, Bond went to space. Purists call it the moment the series left the rails; I call it a film where a man escapes a centrifuge, a gondola drives through St. Mark's Square, and Jaws falls in love. Cinema contains multitudes.

## The plot

A Moonraker space shuttle is stolen mid-transport, and Bond's investigation lands on Hugo Drax — billionaire industrialist, orchid collector, and owner of a space station he neglected to mention to NASA. Drax's plan: wipe out humanity with a nerve toxin and repopulate Earth from orbit with his hand-picked beautiful specimens. Eugenics with a view. It's *that* kind of movie.

## The villain

Michael Lonsdale plays Drax as a bored emperor — soft-spoken, perfectly tailored, and completely unhinged. "Look after Mr. Bond. See that some harm comes to him." His California chateau (actually Vaux-le-Vicomte) and pheasant-shoot assassination attempt on Bond are Moore-era villainy at its most elegant. Jaws returns from *The Spy Who Loved Me* and gets the redemption arc nobody ordered and everybody loved.

## The girl

The Bond girl of the night is **Dr. Holly Goodhead** — CIA agent, shuttle pilot, astrophysicist. Lois Chiles plays her smart and self-possessed; she's onto Bond's cover before he finishes the handshake, and by the finale she's flying the shuttle while he shoots. "Doctor" in the name, competence in the script — for 1979, that's practically a progressive manifesto. Their zero-gravity "re-entry" joke closes the film and the decade.

## The verdict

Is it ridiculous? Devastatingly. Is it fun? Enormously. The laser battle in orbit was silly then and is silly now, but the stunt work — that free-fall opening sequence shot for real — is staggering. Take your medicine and enjoy the spectacle.

**Filed:** 3.5/5 — docked in orbit, martini on the flight deck.
