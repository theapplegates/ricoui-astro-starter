Six years of Bond silence ended with Brosnan walking out of a shadow on a Russian weapons depot in 1986, and the nineties breathed a collective sigh of relief. *GoldenEye* asked what a Cold War spy does after the Cold War ends — and answered: drives a tank through St. Petersburg, apparently.

## The plot

A Soviet weapons test site, a stolen attack helicopter, and GoldenEye — a satellite that fires an electromagnetic pulse capable of frying every circuit in a city. Behind it all: Alec Trevelyan, agent 006, presumed dead by MI6, very much alive and nursing a generational vendetta against Britain. The first film to give Bond a peer opponent: another double-0 who knows all his moves.

## The villain

Sean Bean's Trevelyan is personal — a friend betrayed, a mirror cracked. "I might as well ask you if all those vodka martinis ever silence the screams" is a line that cuts into the character rather than around him. The finale on the giant satellite dish, hanging by one hand over a drop, still rules.

## The girls

Two this time, and both unforgettable in opposite registers. The girl of the night proper is **Natalya Simonova** — Izabella Scorupco as a programmer who survives the slaughter of her own station and keeps up with Bond by sheer competence and nerve. She's the rare Bond girl who is *useful* in the plot and *principled* in her choices.

And then there's **Xenia Onatopp** — Famke Janssen's henchwoman who murders people with her thighs while visibly *enjoying it*. She is absurd, legendary, and easily the most quotable villain-adjacent presence of the Brosnan era.

## The verdict

Also this film gave us the best video game ever made, and this dossier does not grade video games, but it's worth noting. Brosnan's best outing, full stop.

**Filed:** 4.5/5 — for Queen and country, one more martini.
